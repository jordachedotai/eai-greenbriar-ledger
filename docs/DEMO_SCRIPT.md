# Demo script: four minutes in the room

Presenter: Jordache. Matt frames if he is willing; otherwise Devrin. Mock mode on. State loaded: `july`. Signed in as Matt Burke.

## Beat 0: the problem, in his words (20 seconds, Matt or Devrin)
"Every month twelve management teams tell us what they are going to do. Holding them to it depends on remembering what they said in January. I don't." Then: "This is the first time the firm can see, on one screen, whether they did."

## Beat 1: Monday morning (30 seconds)
Portfolio, July. Three companies, twelve initiatives, seven months of cells. Work strip: Need a conversation 1, Slipping 3, Not reported 1, On track 7. "Green means what they said still holds. Amber means a date or a number moved. Red means it moved twice, or by a quarter. Grey means they stopped mentioning it. Every cell is a sentence from a report, with the page number."

## Beat 2: the August report arrives (40 seconds, the wow)
Shift+P, then **August report arrives**. Working indicator. Harlan's ERP row turns red. Strip: Need a conversation 2. "One report came in. The tool read it against the seven before it." Open Harlan. The stack: January "on plan for end of Q2," March "June cutover," May "targeting July," June "phase 2 in Q4," August "phase 2 now Q1 2027." Change chips on May, June, August. "Nobody lied. The date moved three times in five months, one month at a time, and each month it read fine on its own."

## Beat 3: before Thursday's call (30 seconds)
Right rail: three drafted questions, each citing the month it came from. Read the first one aloud. Press **Approve for Thursday**. "He goes into the call with these. He didn't spend Sunday night re-reading eight PDFs."

Point at "What the reports do not say": no cost of delay, no revised budget since March. "It flags the gap. It does not fill it."

## Beat 4: the page number is real (15 seconds)
Click the May cite. The May report opens at page 4 with the sentence on it. Back. "Nothing here is the tool's opinion. It is what management wrote, side by side."

## Beat 5: across companies (40 seconds, James's lens)
Patterns. Card one: Harlan and Corvus are evaluating the same TMS vendors, Corvus is three months ahead. Card three: two go-live dates slipped citing "vendor resourcing," neither report names the vendor. "This is the part a person cannot do. Reading twenty-four reports at once and noticing two companies used the same phrase."

Right rail: the learning log. "James keeps this by hand today, on his own OneDrive, and nobody else can read it. Here it is shared, and each entry is a draft until he confirms it."

## Beat 6: after the call (25 seconds)
Shift+P, **Dictate a note**. The transcript plays. A draft log entry and a current-state line appear on Meridian. Press **Review**. "Two minutes of talking after a plant visit, and the record updates. The learning is kept in his format, not the model's."

## Beat 7: the scale picture (20 seconds, Devrin)
Shift+P, jump to `monday`. Twelve companies. "Three companies today. The portfolio is twelve, and the record starts on day one of the next deal. Every initiative the firm underwrites, tracked from the memo to the exit."

Q&A, "how does it get the reports": "Today a person drops the monthly pack in a folder. In production it watches the SharePoint folder the packs already land in. That is on the connector list for this council."

Q&A, "is it judging management": "No. It shows what they wrote, next to what they wrote before. The judgment is Matt's. Every output is a draft he approves."

Q&A, "what about the numbers": "The financial dashboard is Scott's Fabric work. This reads the words. Matt separated the two himself."

## If time is short
Load `august` and start at Beat 2 with Harlan already open. The stack and the questions are the demo. Never skip the stack.

## What not to say
- No real company names, even as examples. Say "a portfolio company."
- No claim that it reads Greenbriar's SharePoint today. Say "the folder the packs land in."
- No accuracy percentage. Say "every quote links to its page."
- No hours-saved number until Matt gives one. Say "Sunday night" and stop.
- Do not call the flags a score. They are flags.
